Disney Lorcana

Race to collect lore with Disney characters. Easy to pick up, great for families, and deeper than it looks.

60 card deck Max 4 copies Up to 2 inks First to 20 lore
1

Learn how a game works

Ink is your energyOnce per turn, put one card with the ink symbol face down into your inkwell. Each ink card pays for 1 cost.
Play charactersNew characters are "drying" — they can't quest or challenge until your next turn.
Quest for loreExert (turn sideways) a character to gain its lore value. First to 20 lore wins.
ChallengeAttack your opponent's exerted characters to banish them and slow their lore.

Each turn: ready your cards, draw a card (the first player skips this on turn one), then ink, play, quest and challenge in any order.

Shopping Wizard tips (all games)
  • Read the colour-coded message after pasting. Green = added, red = not found. Fix red lines by checking the spelling and the dash between name and version.
  • Set your filters: English language and a minimum condition of Excellent is a good default for playing.
  • Fewer sellers = less postage. Check "Sellers with the most cards" at the bottom of the list — one seller with 90% of your cards often beats ten cheaper ones.
  • You get 10 Shopping Wizard runs per day, so set filters before you run it.
  • Each wants list holds up to 150 entries — plenty for any deck.

Magic: The Gathering

The original trading card game. Huge card pool, lots of formats, and the biggest community out there.

20 life 60 card deck Max 4 copies Commander: 100 singleton
1

Learn how a game works

Lands make manaPlay one land per turn. Tap lands for mana in the five colours to cast your spells.
Creatures fightCreatures can attack from your next turn. Your opponent chooses which creatures block.
Spells do the restInstants can be cast any time — even on your opponent's turn. Sorceries only on your own.
Win the gameReduce your opponent from 20 life to 0 (or they run out of cards to draw).

Each turn: untap, upkeep, draw, main phase, combat, second main phase, end.

Pick a format: Commander (100 different cards, 40 life, 3–4 players) is the most popular casual format. Standard and Pioneer are the classic 1v1 60-card formats.

Once you're hooked

What's a card actually worth?

You don't need this for your first deck — but it'll save you money on your second. Cardmarket and eBay often show different prices for the same card. That's normal: they measure different things.

Cardmarket

Shows the lowest listed price
  • The "From" price is the cheapest copy for sale right now, so it's usually the lowest number you'll see.
  • Prices are in euros, and each seller charges their own postage — a cheap card from a far-off seller can cost more once it arrives.
  • Before you trust a price, filter to English and the condition you want. The cheapest copy is often played or in another language.

eBay

Use the last sold "Buy it now" price
  • Asking prices on eBay mean nothing — anyone can list a card for anything. What matters is what people actually paid.
  • Search the card, then tick Sold items, filter to Buy it now and set Item location to UK only. Auctions can end unusually low or high, and overseas sales can skew the picture.
  • Sold prices are usually higher than Cardmarket, because sellers build in eBay's fees and buyers pay for convenience.
Quick rule: Cardmarket's lowest listing is roughly the floor; eBay's recent sold "Buy it now" prices are roughly the ceiling. A fair price for a trade or sale sits somewhere between the two — and compare three or four recent sales, not just one.
🇬🇧 Buying in the UK: filter to UK sellers

Card prices vary by region. The same card can sell for noticeably different amounts in the UK, Europe and the US, so a price from another country isn't a fair guide to what it's worth here.

  • eBay: on the left-hand filters, set Item location to UK only. Do this when checking sold prices too, or overseas sales will skew the number.
  • Cardmarket: on a card's page, filter Seller location to the United Kingdom. Remember most of Cardmarket's sellers are in Europe and prices are shown in euros, so convert to pounds and add postage before comparing.
  • Buying from UK sellers usually means quicker delivery and simpler postage, even if the card itself is a few pence dearer.
🌍 Cards in other languages: cheaper, but check the rules

Many games let you play official cards printed in other languages, and some languages sell for a lot less than English. Whether that's allowed — and whether it's cheaper — depends on the game:

  • Magic: The Gathering: the official tournament rules allow non-English cards, and the official Oracle text is what counts, not the printed wording. At an event you can ask for a card's official wording, or ask a judge to help translate. Sleeves are optional, but if you use them they must all match. Some languages are much cheaper than English on Cardmarket, though Japanese is often pricier.
  • Disney Lorcana: cards in every official language can be mixed in one deck outside Japan and China, as long as they're sleeved so you can't tell them apart.
  • Riftbound: the official tournament rules allow official cards in any language, and Chinese cards often sell for less than English. If you sleeve your cards, the sleeves must all match and have opaque backs (sleeves are required at bigger events). At an event you can ask a judge for any card's official wording in your preferred language.

The catch: you need to know what your cards do, and your opponent is entitled to know too. Bring a printout or app with the English text, and use matching opaque sleeves. While you're still learning the game, English cards make life a lot easier.
